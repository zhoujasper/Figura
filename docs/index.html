Figura Guide
Open Figura

Figura

A canvas in your browser for diagrams of every kind: automata, category diagrams, circuits, geometric constructions, ZX-calculus. Draw it once, paste clean TikZ into your paper.

Open Figura Getting started

What you can do

Shapes that fit their text

Circles, boxes, diamonds, triangles, dots and pictures. A shape grows with what you write in it and shrinks back.

Maths everywhere

$…$, \[…\] and align in shapes, labels and lines, set in the same fonts as your paper.

Lines of every kind

Arcs, Bézier curves, elbows; double, wavy or zigzag; many arrow heads, loops, rays, labels you can drag.

Wire fast

Many to one in a click, a stroke through shapes, parallel lines, ports that stay where you put them.

Constructions

Midpoints, perpendiculars, intersections and compass circles that follow the points they are built on.

TikZ in, TikZ out

Export code that compiles with only \usepackage{tikz}, and paste TikZ back in to keep editing.

No install, no account

Figura is a single web page. Your drawings are saved in your browser as you work, and the whole thing runs offline once it has loaded. It works with a mouse, a trackpad, a pen or your finger.

Its sibling project is ZX Draw; the two share their look and most of their habits.

Getting started

Your first diagram in a minute, and a map of the screen.

Your first diagram

  1. Press O and click the canvas twice, some way apart. You now have two circles.
  2. Double-click each circle and type $q_0$ and $q_1$. Press Esc or click away when done.
  3. Press E for the connector, then drag from one circle to the other. Double-click the line and type its label.
  4. Press Mod S, keep the TikZ tab and press Copy. Paste it into your .tex file.

Moving around

  • Pan: hold Space and drag, use the hand tool H, or scroll with two fingers.
  • Zoom: Mod + scroll, pinch, or = and −. F fits everything on screen.
  • Minimap: click or drag in it to jump.

Selecting

  • Click to select, Shift-click to add, drag on empty canvas for a box.
  • Press V again to switch the box to a lasso; hold Alt while dragging to flip it for one go.
  • Mod A selects everything on the page.

On a phone

The toolbar sits at the bottom and panels open as sheets. Tap to select, drag to move, pinch to zoom, and press and hold anywhere for the menu that a right-click gives on a computer.

Press ? in Figura at any time for the built-in guide, with a looping demo for every gesture.

Shapes

Circles, boxes, triangles, diamonds and dots, drawn like in any design tool.

Drawing a shape

ToolKey
CircleO
BoxR
TriangleY
DiamondD
DotP

Click to place a shape at its default size, or drag to draw its box. While dragging:

  • Shift keeps it in proportion: a square, a round circle, a square diamond, an equilateral triangle.
  • Alt draws from the centre outward.
  • Space lets you move the shape before letting go.

A new shape takes the look you last used for that kind, so a row of states needs no restyling.

Resize and rotate

A selected shape shows eight handles and a rotate knob. Shift keeps proportions while resizing, Alt resizes around the centre. Rotation is drawn softly to multiples of 15°; with Shift it steps. [ and ] rotate by 15°.

Fill and border

The tool row sets the look of new shapes, the style bar changes selected ones. Both offer:

  • Fill: quick colours, none, or any colour from the picker (square, wheel or sliders; type #hex, rgb(), hsl() or a CSS name).
  • Border: width, solid / dashed / dotted, double, colour or none.
  • Corners: rounded boxes with an exact radius.
  • Triangle direction and diamond shape (square, wide, tall).

Right-click any quick colour, width or style preset to edit, move or remove it. The + at the end of a row keeps the current look as a new preset.

Dots

Dots are small filled circles for graph vertices and geometry. Click to place one, snapped to the grid. Their sizes are presets in the tool row; a selected dot has an exact size stepper.

Moving and copying

  • Drag to move. On the grid the shape's centre snaps; in free mode guides show when edges and centres line up.
  • Alt + drag leaves a copy behind. Mod D duplicates.
  • Arrow keys nudge. W swaps two selected shapes, each taking over the other's lines.

Text and maths

Write inside shapes, beside them, on lines or anywhere on the canvas, with LaTeX maths.

Writing

  • In a shape: double-click it, or select it and press Enter. The shape grows to fit.
  • On the canvas: double-click empty space, or use the text tool T. Text left empty is removed.
  • Formatting: Mod B, I, U; size and colour in the style bar; vertical text in the text panel.

Maths

Anything between dollars is typeset with KaTeX on the canvas and in the same fonts as LaTeX on export.

WriteFor
$x^2$ or \(x^2\)Inline maths
$$…$$ or \[…\]Display maths
\begin{align}…\end{align}Aligned lines (also gather, equation)

Letters such as π or → typed directly are turned into \pi and \to in the export, so pdflatex never chokes on them.

Labels outside a shape

Double-click just outside a shape's side to write a label there, or use the label button in the style bar. Drag a label around the shape to any angle; it settles on the middle of a side when close. The angle is kept in the TikZ export.

Labels on lines

Double-click a line where the label should go. Drag the label along the line (it snaps to the middle) or across it to put it on one side, on the line, or on the other side.

Lines and arrows

The connector E draws lines between shapes, from shapes to empty space, or between two free points.

Drawing a line

  • Drag from a shape to another shape, or into empty space for a loose end.
  • Click, click: click a shape or a spot, then a second one. The same shape twice makes a self-loop.
  • Hover dots: with the select tool, hovering a shape shows small dots on its sides. Drag one to start a line; drop it on empty canvas, or just click it, to make a connected copy of the shape.

Paths

Select a line to see its handles: drag the middle of an arc to bend it, the two square handles of a Bézier, or the step of an elbow. Straighten and Reverse are in the style bar.

Line styles

Any style goes with any path and any arrow head. Width, dash pattern and colour have editable presets like the shape styles.

Arrow heads

Each end has its own head: none, arrow, filled, open triangle, double, dot, open dot, bar, hook or ray. The tool row offers common pairs (arrow, both ways, inheritance, maps-to, …); the ⋯ button opens every head for each end.

A ray end runs on past its point; on the canvas it goes to the edge, in exports it fades out after one grid step.

Self-loops

Click the same shape twice with the connector. Figura picks a free side; drag the loop round the shape to move it.

Moving a line's ends

With a shape selected, a dot sits where each of its lines meets it. Drag a dot along the outline, onto another shape, or off into empty space to free that end.

Ports and fast wiring

Line ends that stay put, and ways to draw many lines at once.

Ports

Every line end on a box, triangle or diamond sits on a side and stays there when shapes move. Ends on one side spread out evenly, and a side that gets crowded makes the shape grow. Diamonds wire to their corners.

Right-click a shape to choose how its ports are laid out: evenly, packed to the ends of each side, or all in the middle.

Along the line

Circles work the other way by default: lines aim at the centre and meet the outline where they cross it, as you expect in an automaton. Each shape can be switched in the style bar or its menu (Lines meet): along the line, or fixed ports.

Many to one

  1. Select two or more shapes.
  2. Pick the connector and click the target. Every selected shape is wired to it.
  • Shift + click keeps the sources selected for the next target.
  • Mod + click adds or removes a source.
  • Holding S in the select tool turns on the connector until you let go, with the selection as sources; one shape is enough.

Chains and parallel lines

  • Drag through several shapes with the connector: they are wired in the order you pass them.
  • Hold P with shapes selected and click a target: parallel lines, each source to the shape at the same offset. A quick tap of P is still the dot tool.
  • Right-drag in the connector box-selects, so you can pick sources without switching tools.

Seeing connections

Hold Mod for a moment with shapes selected: the shapes wired to each one pulse, one colour per selected shape.

Deleting a shape in a wire

Delete a shape that has exactly two lines and they join into one, keeping the direction. A new straight line bends just enough to miss shapes in its way.

Geometry

Constructions that stay true: move a point and everything built on it follows.

Constructions

Select the objects, then press the key or right-click and pick from the menu, which lists only what the selection allows.

Built points are dots and cannot be dragged; they fade out when their objects stop meeting. Make free in the menu cuts the tie. Deleting an object deletes what was built on it.

Compass

Press C. Press on a centre and drag out the radius, or click the centre and then a point on the circle. A circle built on shapes follows them.

Grid

The switch at the top (or G) cycles dots → half steps → free. Turning the grid on offers to snap everything that is off it in one step.

Pictures

Put images on the canvas or inside shapes.

  • Add: the picture tool I, paste an image, or drop a file on the canvas.
  • Fill a shape: drop a picture onto it, or use the picture button in its style bar.
  • Crop: on a picture the handles move the frame while the image stays put, and dragging moves the image inside. In a shape, drag to move it and scroll to zoom; it always covers the shape.

Large images are scaled down to 1800 px. In the TikZ export each picture becomes an \includegraphics with a cropped PNG you download from the export window.

Pages, layers and view

Organise many diagrams and present them.

Pages

  • The + beside the tabs adds a page. Double-click a tab to rename it; right-click for duplicate, move and close.
  • Every page is saved in your browser automatically.

Lock

The lock beside the zoom bar (K) freezes a page: dragging only pans and nothing can be edited. Handy while presenting.

Layers and alignment

With several shapes selected the style bar aligns them (left, centre, right, top, middle, bottom) and spaces three or more evenly.

Laser pointer

Press L to point at things while you share your screen. Dot is a glowing pointer; Line leaves a trail that fades. Click the tool again to switch.

Your own toolbar

Keep the tools you use and add buttons that draw exactly what you need.

Layouts

Figura comes with three toolbars, chosen in Settings or by right-clicking the toolbar:

LayoutHolds

ZX-calculusZ and X spiders, Hadamard box, wire points, boxes, wires and Hadamard edges.
Category theoryObjects, arrows, mono ↪, epi ↠, dashed, maps-to ↦, 2-cells ⇒ and equals.

Changing a built-in layout makes your own copy, so the original is always there.

Own buttons

A button stores a whole look: a shape with its fill, border, size and starting text, or a line with its heads, dashes and colour. Give it a key and it beats the built-in one while the layout is on.

  1. Style a shape or line the way you like and select it.
  2. Right-click the toolbar and pick Add the selected look as a button.
  3. Click the new button again (while it is active) to rename it, give it a key or fine-tune it.

The editor

Customize opens the editor: drag buttons to reorder, pull one off the strip to hide it, drag new ones in from the row below. A built-in tool shows the parts of its tool row, which you can hide or reorder too.

Sharing

Export a layout as a .figtool file and import it on another computer, or send it to a co-author. Importing never overwrites your own layouts.

Reordering anything

Hold Mod and drag any toolbar button, top-bar button, option row or quick colour to reorder it. The order is remembered.

Shortcuts

Open help (?) → Shortcuts, click an action and press a key. A key already in use swaps over.

TikZ export

Plain TikZ that compiles with nothing but \usepackage{tikz}.

Exporting

  1. Press Mod S (or the export button) and stay on the TikZ tab.
  2. Choose Whole page or Selection.
  3. Copy, or download a .tex file. If the drawing has pictures, download them too.

Mod C on a selection also puts its TikZ on your clipboard, ready to paste into your editor.

In your document

The export starts with a \usetikzlibrary line naming only the libraries it needs, so the pasted block works as it is:

\documentclass{article}
\usepackage{tikz}
\usepackage{amsmath} % if your labels use align and friends

\begin{document}
% paste the exported block here
\end{document}

An exported picture looks like this:

% Figura · needs \usepackage{tikz} \usetikzlibrary{arrows.meta}
\usetikzlibrary{arrows.meta}
\begin{tikzpicture}[
  x=0.66667pt,
  y=-0.66667pt,
  oval/.style={…},
  link/.style={…}]
  \definecolor{fgInk}{HTML}{1D1D1F}
  \node[oval] (a) at (0,0) {$q_0$};
  \node[oval] (b) at (160,0) {$q_1$};
  \draw[link] (a) to[bend left=20] node[lab] {$a$} (b);
  ...
\end{tikzpicture}

The first line says what the block needs. Options shared by several shapes or lines are pulled into short styles at the top (oval, box, link, lab…), colours are defined once by name, and shapes are real TikZ nodes, so the code stays easy to tweak by hand. One canvas pixel is ⅔ pt and y points down, as on screen.

Back into Figura

Paste TikZ onto the canvas, or open a .tex file, and Figura reads it back: nodes, lines, bends, loops, labels, arrow tips, colours (red!30, rgb, \definecolor) and styles from \tikzset. Code that Figura wrote comes back exactly as it was.

figura.sty

A short style file for documents with many diagrams, or for writing them by hand.

Setup

Download figura.sty from the export window (or the repository) and put it next to your .tex file. The download also defines your own quick colours.

\usepackage{figura}

\begin{figura}            % 1 unit = 1 canvas pixel, y points down
  \node[fg box, fill=fgRose] (a) at (0,0)   {Hello};
  \node[fg circle]           (b) at (160,0) {$q_0$};
  \draw[fg line, ->] (a) -- node[fg label] {$f$} (b);
  \draw[fg line, ->] (b) to[loop at=90] (b);
\end{figura}

Choose the figura.sty tab in the export window to get this shorter form.

Opening

Mod O or the folder button opens .figura, .tex and .figtool files. Opened pages are added beside the ones you have, never replacing them. You can also drop a file on the window.

Where your work lives

Pages are saved in this browser as you go. To move to another computer or browser, or to keep a backup, export a .figura file.

Clearing the browser's site data removes the saved pages. Keep a .figura file of anything that matters.
